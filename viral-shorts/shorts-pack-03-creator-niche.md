## Short 1: "I started a Shorts channel from zero. Here's what flopped."

**Titles:** "My First Short Flopped. Here's Why." / "3 Mistakes That Killed My First Short"

> I made a Shorts channel from nothing. The first video flopped. [0-5s]
> Mistake one: the topic. It sounded smart, but people open Shorts for a quick dopamine hit, not to feel doomed. [5-15s]

## Short 2: "The only thing that makes a Short go viral"

**Titles:** "Make Them Watch. Keep Them Watching." / "The Shorts Algorithm in 40 Seconds"

> Everyone asks how the Shorts algorithm works. It's simpler than you think. [0-6s]
> Two jobs. One: make people stop scrolling. Two: keep them watching. [6-14s]
> Hook job: state a problem or a stake in the first sentence. "You run a company, and you have a problem." [14-24s]
> Keep-watching job: every few seconds, something changes. A cut, a reveal, a new image, a new sound. [24-34s]
> Check your analytics: if people leave in the first 3 seconds, rewrite the hook. If they leave in the middle, add change. [34-46s]
> Every other trick is a version of these two jobs. [46-52s]

## Short 3: "Copy a viral structure without copying the video"

**Titles:** "The Viral Script Trick Nobody Explains" / "How to Reverse-Engineer Any Viral Short"

> Here's how to borrow a viral Short without stealing it. [0-5s]
> Step one: take the transcript of a Short that already went viral. [5-12s]
> Step two: don't copy the words. Mark the structure. Hook, problem, steps, reveal. [12-22s]
> Step three: write a new video on a different topic using that same skeleton. [22-31s]
> Step four: make it yours, with your own examples, your own jokes, your own visuals. [31-40s]
> The structure is what made people stay. The topic and the voice are yours. [40-48s]
> Do that, and you're learning from the algorithm instead of fighting it. [48-54s]

(Learn from structure only. Don't reuse someone's actual script, footage, or art.)

## Short 4: "Visuals beat script (the week I learned that)"

**Titles:** "Why My Script Was Right and My Short Still Flopped" / "It Wasn't the Script"

> I used the exact same script structure as a channel that goes viral. My short still flopped. [0-8s]
> I put them side by side, and watched like a doomscroller. [8-14s]
> Their video had wild animation. Mine had random clips and basic slides. [14-22s]
> The script gets them in. The visuals keep them. [22-30s]
> So I switched to AI video: write a storyboard, generate images, animate each shot. [30-40s]
> You still have to do the thinking. AI won't write the shot list for you. [40-48s]
> Better visuals, same script, a different result. [48-54s]

## Short 5: "I did everything right and felt nothing"

**Titles:** "The Secret Behind Viral Shorts Isn't the Algorithm" / "Why I Stopped Chasing Views"

> I spent a week copying structures, hooks, trends. I did everything the algorithm wanted. [0-8s]
> And something was still missing. [8-12s]
> When I looked back, my best old videos had one thing in common: I made them because I thought they were fun. [12-24s]
> Chasing a number made the videos worse. Making something I'd actually watch made them better. [24-34s]
> Use the formulas. They help. But if you hate making the video, viewers can feel it. [34-45s]
> Make the thing you'd stop scrolling for. [45-50s]
