# Viral Shorts Pack 02: Isaac's exact niche and topics

Niche: "weird business or government story that sounds dumb, then feels genius". These are the three topics from his video, written as original scripts in the "you're the character with a problem" structure that worked for his final short.

Format: 9:16, about 45-55s, 110-130 spoken words, low-poly 3D look, fast 3-4 word captions, SFX on every cut.

**Fact-check before posting.** These come from general knowledge, not live research. Items marked (verify) are the ones I'm least sure of.

## Short 1: How Nescafe got Japan to drink coffee

**Titles:** "Japan Didn't Drink Coffee. Then One Company Did This." / "The Sneaky Plan That Made Japan Love Coffee"

> You're a giant coffee company, and you have a problem. A whole country doesn't drink coffee. Japan drinks tea. [0-8s]
> Ads won't work. Tea is part of their culture. [8-13s]
> So you skip the adults and start with the kids. [13-18s]
> You launch coffee-flavored candy and desserts. Sweet, harmless, everywhere. (verify exact products and dates) [18-27s]
> A kid who grows up loving the taste of coffee doesn't need convincing at 25. [27-35s]
> Next, you meet adults where they already are: gift culture. Coffee sets become the polite thing to give. (verify) [35-44s]
> A few decades later, Japan is one of the biggest coffee markets on Earth. [44-50s]
> It was Nescafe, playing the long game the whole time. [50-55s]

## Short 2: Why Yamaha makes everything

**Titles:** "The Company That Makes Pianos AND Motorcycles" / "Why Yamaha Makes Bathtubs"

> You run a piano and organ company. After the war, your factory is nearly empty, and the machines are sitting idle. [0-8s]
> You can't just let them rot. [8-12s]
> So you ask a weird question: what else can these machines build? [12-18s]
> Metalworking skills turn into motorcycles. Yamaha Motor is born in 1955. [18-26s]
> Fiberglass experience from boats turns into jet skis, and into bathtubs. (verify) [26-35s]
> Wood and sound know-how already makes flutes, guitars and drums. [35-43s]
> The rule: never sell a product. Sell what your factory knows how to do. [43-50s]
> That's why one company makes both a grand piano and a jet ski. [50-55s]

## Short 3: The Brazilian prison where reading cuts your sentence

**Titles:** "In Brazil, Prisoners Can Read Their Way Out" / "The Prison Rule That Rewards Reading"

> You're running Brazil's prison system, and you have a problem. Prisons are overcrowded, and most inmates leave with nothing to do except offend again. [0-9s]
> You can't build prisons fast enough. [9-13s]
> So you try something strange. Read a book, write a report on it, and get days cut from your sentence. [13-22s]
> The program has caps: a limited number of books per year, and a fixed number of days off per book. (verify exact numbers: about 4 days per book, up to about 48 days per year) [22-32s]
> Inmates get a reason to engage. Teachers get a real way to measure learning. [32-40s]
> The state gets lower cost per prisoner and fewer people going back. [40-46s]
> It's a government acting like a business: reward the behavior you want. [46-52s]
