# Viral Shorts Pack 01: "You're the CEO" business stories

Built on the structure of the short that finally worked in Isaac's "I Blew Up a Shorts Channel in 7 Days" (youtu.be/QXfugR3ZIAs):

1. **Viewer is the character** with a problem stated in the first sentence.
2. **Solution unfolds step by step**, with each step a small surprise.
3. **Ending reveals the real story** ("it was X doing this the whole time") and gives a payoff, not a joke.
4. Topic = something that sounds dumb until explained, then feels genius.

Format for all three: 9:16, 40-50 seconds, about 110-130 spoken words, low-poly 3D art style, fast captions (3-4 words at a time), a sound effect on every scene change.

Caveat: web research returned no reliable "trending right now" data and the VidIQ account had no credits left. Topics are evergreen true stories, not trend-matched. Fact-check numbers before posting.

## Short 1: Costco's $1.50 hot dog

**Title options:** "Costco Loses Money on This On Purpose" / "Why Costco Will Never Raise This Price"

**Script**

> You run Costco. Your hot dog combo costs a dollar fifty, and it has since 1985. [0-6s]
> Hot dogs, buns and soda all got more expensive. Your accountants are screaming: raise the price. [6-14s]
> But you don't. Instead, you build bigger food courts. [14-19s]
> Why? Because nobody comes to Costco for a hot dog. They come because of the hot dog. [19-27s]
> It's the one price every member remembers. It proves you're not ripping them off, so they trust everything else on the shelf. [27-37s]
> The same trick works for the four-dollar-ninety-nine rotisserie chicken. [37-42s]
> The hot dog isn't food. It's the cheapest billboard in retail. [42-48s]
> It's called a loss leader, and Costco's founder reportedly told his CEO: "If you raise the effing hot dog price, I'll kill you." [48-55s]

(Trim the last line if over 50s.)

## Short 2: McDonald's is not a burger company

**Title options:** "McDonald's Doesn't Make Money From Burgers" / "The Real McDonald's Business"

**Script**

> You own a burger chain. You sell cheap burgers, and you're barely breaking even. [0-6s]
> Your franchisees make the money. You just get a small fee. [6-12s]
> Then a financial guy named Harry Sonneborn shows up with a weird idea: stop selling burgers to your franchisees. [12-20s]
> Sell them land. Buy the property under every restaurant, then lease it back to them. [20-29s]
> Now it doesn't matter if burgers sell well or badly. Every month, rent comes in. And the more locations open, the more land you own. [29-40s]
> Today McDonald's is one of the largest real estate holders in the world. [40-46s]
> Burgers are just how they get people to show up on the property. [46-52s]

**Shot list**

| # | Image prompt | Animation |

| 2 | Low-poly franchisee holding a bag of money, owner holding a few coins | Bag swings, coins drop |
| 3 | Low-poly suited man with a clipboard pointing at the ground beneath the restaurant | Camera tilts down to reveal the land |
| 4 | Low-poly map of the world filling with golden plots under red-and-yellow arches | Map fills region by region |
| 5 | Low-poly landlord counting rent while burgers fly through the background | Burgers orbit |

## Short 3: De Beers made engagement rings a rule

**Title options:** "A Company Invented a Tradition and You Fell For It" / "The Lie Behind Every Engagement Ring"

**Script**

> You run a diamond company. There's one problem: nobody needs diamonds. Sales are collapsing. [0-7s]
> You can't lower the price. If diamonds look cheap, your whole stockpile loses value. [7-14s]
> So you don't change the diamond. You change the story. [14-19s]
> You hire an ad agency. Their idea: make a diamond ring the only acceptable proof of love. [19-27s]
> You put diamonds on movie stars. You feed magazines "how much should he spend" rules. You invent the slogan: "A Diamond Is Forever." [27-38s]
> And because it's forever, you never resell it, so supply stays tight and prices stay high. [38-46s]
> Within a few decades, the "tradition" felt ancient. It was a marketing campaign from the 1940s. [46-54s]
> The company was De Beers. [54-56s]

## Production checklist (same for all three)

1. **Voiceover**: ElevenLabs (or any TTS). Pace slightly fast. Cut every silence.
2. **Visuals**: generate each image from the prompts above in one consistent low-poly style, then animate each as a 3-5s clip. Keep the character design consistent.
3. **Edit**: keyframes for movement, fast captions, trending music at low volume, SFX on every cut.
4. **Hook check**: the first sentence must state a problem the viewer owns ("You run...").
5. **Payoff check**: the last 5 seconds must reveal the answer. No joke ending.
6. **Post** on Shorts, TikTok and Instagram at the same time. Review swipe-away rate after 24 hours. Target is above about 80% staying and about 90% retention to compare with Isaac's benchmarks.
7. **If one flops**, change the topic or the hook, not the format.
